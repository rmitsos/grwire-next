**INSIGHT · ENERGY & DIGITAL INFRASTRUCTURE**

# Over 12 GW of data center requests at ADMIE, 517 MW contracted. What Greece's new grid rules change

When data centers in Greece are discussed, the focus is usually on fibre rings and subsea cables. In practice, the bottleneck is not telecoms — it is power-grid capacity. In November 2026 a new legal framework for connecting data centers to the Greek grid entered into force, introducing a €200,000 per MW bank guarantee, a 60% renewable-power mandate, and an annual levy for host communities. This piece puts the figures side by side to show what actually changes.

---

## 1. The gap between applications and contracted projects

In late September 2026, connection requests for data centers in ADMIE's queue were already reported at around 5 GW (energypress.gr, 22/09/2026). Within weeks, total applications surpassed 12 GW — roughly equal to Greece's entire peak national electricity load — while projects with signed contracts or confirmed connection offers stood at 517 MW (en.parapolitika.gr, 08/11/2026).

That is a ratio of more than 23 to 1. Many of those applications do not represent funded projects ready to break ground, but attempts to reserve scarce grid capacity ahead of tighter rules.

*How much of the 12 GW queue has a real customer and financing behind it?*

## 2. What the new framework requires

The Ministry of Environment and Energy's bill (YPEN, 10/10/2026), which entered into force in November (Balkan Green Energy News, 13/11/2026), sets four clear thresholds for new projects:

| Rule | Threshold | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Connection bank guarantee (3-year) | **€200,000 / MW** | Balkan Green Energy News, 12/10/2026 |
| Minimum annual renewable power | **60% RES** | YPEN, 10/10/2026 |
| Annual host-community levy | **€20,000 / MW** (75% municipalities, 25% consumers) | Balkan Green Energy News, 13/11/2026 |
| Operator split (ADMIE / DEDDIE) | **Above 10 MW** at ADMIE, below at DEDDIE | Balkan Green Energy News, 12/10/2026 |

ADMIE is also required to publish periodic regional grid-availability maps and account for data centers separately in its ten-year development plan. Developers cover the grid connection and reinforcement works required by their projects.

*Is €200,000 per MW the right filter to clear the queue without slowing down serious builders?*

## 3. How the map shifts

For a 100 MW project, the guarantee reaches €20M and the annual community levy €2M. For a 300 MW campus — such as the site PPC is planning in Western Macedonia, with potential to scale toward 1 GW (en.parapolitika.gr, 08/11/2026) — the guarantee equates to €60M.

That shifts where projects can realistically be built. Attica hosts most of Greece's existing data centers and fibre interconnection points, but its substations are already under pressure. Regions with available land, existing high-voltage substations, and renewable generation — such as the former lignite basins of Western Macedonia — gain a clear practical advantage.

*Can large sites outside Attica serve latency-sensitive workloads, or are they mainly a fit for AI training capacity?*

## 4. Telecoms and power at the same table

Until recently, building a data center in Greece was mainly a telecoms and real-estate decision: land close to fibre rings and subsea landing stations. Under the new rules, electricity becomes the binding factor that decides whether and where a site gets built.

Any large developer now needs long-term renewable supply for at least 60% of consumption, balance-sheet capacity for the bank guarantee, and access to an uncongested substation. That puts power utilities and energy groups at the centre of Greece's digital infrastructure build-out — either as partners or as direct developers.

*Over the next two years, will independent data center operators still drive new capacity, or will energy-backed partnerships dominate?*
