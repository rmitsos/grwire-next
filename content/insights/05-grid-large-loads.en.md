**INSIGHT · ENERGY & INFRASTRUCTURE**

# Data centres have asked Greece's grid for 9 to 12 GW. About 517 MW has a signed connection

Requests to connect data centres to Greece's transmission grid have grown from about 1.2 GW at the end of 2025 to between 9 and 12 GW this autumn, depending on the count. Only about 517 MW has a signed contract or final connection offer. A draft bill now puts a price on getting in line: a €200,000-per-MW guarantee and a turnover test that most developers will not pass. This piece looks at what is holding connections back, and what it will cost whoever is trying to build.

---

## 1. The queue is many times what has been signed

Data-centre connection requests to IPTO (ΑΔΜΗΕ), the transmission operator, reached 5 GW in September, up from 1.2 GW at the end of 2025 (insider.gr, 21/09/2026), and 9 GW by early October (energypress.gr, 07/10/2026). A report in early November puts applications above 12 GW, with projects holding signed contracts or final connection offers at 517 MW (Greek City Times, 02/11/2026). The counts differ by date and source, but the ratio is the point: for every megawatt with a signed connection, there are well over ten waiting.

*How much of a queue that size is real projects, and how much is a place held in line?*

## 2. Why the queue stalls

Requests are assessed in the order they arrive. When IPTO reaches one it cannot serve because the network in that area is saturated, it cannot move on to the next (insider.gr, 21/09/2026). Attica now has "minimal to zero" headroom for new data centres beyond those already licensed (energypress.gr, 07/10/2026). A PwC roadmap cited in the same report puts indicative headroom at 630 MW in Western Macedonia, 605 MW in Thessaly, 485 MW in Central Macedonia and 480 MW in Central Greece, against 180 MW in Attica.

IPTO's own proposals: let it skip requests in saturated areas, keep a register of investors at the regulator (ΡΑΑΕΥ), and ask for an environmental licence and proof of land before an application counts (insider.gr, 21/09/2026). The one large project in our sources that has gone where the capacity is, PPC's (ΔΕΗ) data centre with AWS, is planned at 300 MW in Western Macedonia, with possible expansion to 1 GW (same source).

*If the capacity is in Western Macedonia and Thessaly, and the demand is in Attica, who moves?*

## 3. What it will cost to get in line

The ministry's draft bill, in public consultation in October, sets the price of a place in the queue (Business Daily, 17/10/2026; Balkan Green Energy News, 12/10/2026):

- **Guarantee:** a €200,000-per-MW bank guarantee to request a final connection offer, valid for at least three years, cut by 25% when the connection contract is signed. That is €2 million for 10 MW, €20 million for 100 MW and €60 million for the 300 MW PPC/AWS project in Kozani.
- **Turnover test:** for connection to the transmission grid, annual turnover in at least one of the last three years must exceed 30% of the project's construction cost, taken at €10 million per MW. On our arithmetic from that rule, a 100 MW data centre implies a €1 billion build and a developer with more than €300 million in turnover.
- **Who handles what:** IPTO above 10 MW, HEDNO (ΔΕΔΔΗΕ) below.
- **Renewables:** at least 60% of annual consumption from renewable generation, from day one.
- **Priority:** projects above 200 MW get priority if 80% of their power comes from a distributed producer or from storage with six hours' duration.
- **Grid map:** IPTO classifies extra-high-voltage substations as free, partially congested or fully congested, updated every two months.

The renewables rule has its own cost: Pantelis Biskas, quoted by Greek City Times, estimates that a 1,000 MW site would need 3 to 4 GW of new solar (02/11/2026). We have not confirmed that the bill has passed in this form.

*Does a €20 million guarantee and a €300 million turnover bar clean up the queue, or hand it to the five largest players?*

## 4. Where the grid is heading

IPTO's draft ten-year development plan for 2027–2036 totals €9 billion, with about €7 billion of it by 2030, across 110 transmission projects, 15 of them new (insider.gr, 14/09/2026). The coverage we found is about interconnections and island links; it does not say how much of the plan adds capacity for new large loads. The plan went to consultation until 10/10/2026 and still needs the regulator's approval.

We looked for figures on other large industrial users competing for the same transmission capacity and did not find any we could source. For now the competing demand on record is the data-centre queue itself.

*If €7 billion of grid work lands by 2030, how much of it is substation and line capacity where the data centres want to be, and who builds it?*
