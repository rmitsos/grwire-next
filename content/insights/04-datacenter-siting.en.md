**INSIGHT · ENERGY & INFRASTRUCTURE**

# Across the line from Memphis, an AI data centre runs its own gas power plant. In Attica, residents are taking far smaller data centres to the Council of State

In Mississippi, regulators approved 41 gas turbines to power xAI's Colossus 2 data centre, over objections from the people who live around them. In Attica, data centres of 12.5 to 80 MW are drawing negative municipal opinions and a Council of State challenge before they are even running. This piece compares how the two places handle the same question: who carries the local cost of a data centre.

---

## 1. Mississippi: the turbines ran first, the permit came after

On 10/03/2026 Mississippi regulators approved an air permit for xAI to run 41 gas turbines at a site in Southaven, just across the state line from Memphis, to power its Colossus 2 data centre (SELC, 10/03/2026). The Southern Environmental Law Center, which opposed the permit, says xAI was already running up to 27 turbines there without a permit, in breach of the Clean Air Act, and that the plant will release smog-forming pollution, fine particles (PM2.5) and formaldehyde. SELC cites a study putting the health damage from the particle pollution at $30–44 million a year. On 09/04/2026 SELC appealed the permit on behalf of the NAACP, Young, Gifted & Green and the Safe and Sound Coalition (WLBT, 09/04/2026). We found no report of the appeal's outcome.

Several tech outlets report that some of these turbines are GE CF6 aircraft engines, the type used on the Boeing 747, rebuilt by ProEnergy to run on natural gas (Crypto Briefing). We could not confirm this from xAI, the regulator or SELC, so we treat it as press reporting, not established fact.

*If a data centre can bring its own power plant and get the permit afterwards, who decides what the neighbourhood breathes?*

## 2. Attica: the objections come before the first server

In Spata, residents of Christoupoli have asked the Council of State to review the act that puts data centres under 20 MW in a lighter licensing category with no full environmental impact study (infocom.gr, 06/12/2025). Their concerns are noise (they want a 50 dB limit), heat from the cooling systems and the effect on the local microclimate; one planned site sits about 140 metres from the settlement. The Spata–Artemida municipal council rejected the environmental study for Project Olive, an 80 MW, €300 million data centre for Apto and Dromeus. The Attica Regional Council approved the same study by majority, with conditions, and the final decision passed to the Decentralised Administration of Attica. Microsoft's 25 MW data centre is under construction in the same area, alongside Data In Scale (PPC–Damac), 12.5 MW and expandable to 25 MW (same source).

In Kryoneri, the Municipality of Dionysos gave a negative opinion on the environmental study for a planned data centre, citing how close it is to homes and schools, noise, and its water and power needs (Marathon Press, 23/10/2026). A negative opinion does not cancel a project on its own.

*Is a negative municipal opinion a brake, or only a delay?*

## 3. In Greece the brake is the grid, and it is getting tight

Greek data centres connect to the grid, and the grid is rationed. Requests from data centres to connect to the transmission system (IPTO/ΑΔΜΗΕ) reached 9 GW by October 2026, up from about 5 GW in mid-June, and Attica has "minimal to zero" headroom for new data centres beyond those already licensed (energypress.gr, 07/10/2026). A PwC roadmap cited in the same report puts indicative headroom at 630 MW in Western Macedonia and 605 MW in Thessaly, against 180 MW in Attica. The energy ministry plans to change the queue from "first come, first served" towards "first ready, first served."

The national power company is not a bystander either: PPC (ΔΕΗ) is itself a partner in Data In Scale in Spata. That is the opposite of the Mississippi model, where the data centre built its own plant. But if Attica runs out of grid, the pressure to generate on site will grow. None of our sources shows a Greek developer planning that today.

*When the grid around Athens is full, will developers move to where the capacity is, or bring generation to the site?*

## 4. What this does and doesn't show

We found no Greek data centre that runs on on-site gas generation as its main supply. We are not claiming Greece is more environmentally careful in general. What the sources show is narrower: the Greek objections run through formal channels (municipal opinions, environmental studies, the Council of State) against projects of 12.5 to 80 MW, and grid access is allocated by the transmission operator. The Council of State case in Spata is undecided.

*Which is the better safeguard: a stricter permit, or a grid that simply says no?*
