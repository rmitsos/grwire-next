**INSIGHT · TELECOM & MARKETS**

# Starlink in Greece: growing or slowing? What the numbers show

Starlink has become one of the most talked-about subjects in the Greek telecom market. In recent weeks two pictures have been circulating: one has the satellite service passing 200,000 subscribers and putting pressure on the operators; the other, from OTE CEO Kostas Nebis, says its growth has slowed since early 2026. We looked at what rests on data and what rests on estimates. The answer is that both pictures can be true at once, and that there is no official number yet.

---

## 1. What we know for certain

SpaceX does not publish subscribers by country, and EETT has not released a separate figure for satellite connections in 2026. So any Starlink subscriber number for Greece is an estimate.

The firmest data point comes from Ookla: about 6% of fixed internet speed tests in Greece in Q1 2026 were run over Starlink, second in Europe after Bulgaria. That shows real presence, but it is a share of speed-test samples, not market share. The company is also preparing two ground stations, in Thessaloniki and Preveza, with 2027 as the target for operation. The Preveza station already has a construction licence from EETT.

*What would change in the debate if there were an official number?*

## 2. The market estimates

Through 2026 market estimates hovered around 100,000 active connections. More recently, estimates above 200,000 have also been heard. The gap is large: to double its base within a few months, Starlink would have to be growing faster than ever. Until there is a second, independent sign, the estimate that can be supported most comfortably is "more than 100,000".

*How certain should a number be before it becomes a headline?*

## 3. OTE's view

Presenting nine-month results (30/10/2026), Kostas Nebis acknowledged that Starlink's arrival three years ago cost OTE customers, mainly in areas where copper gave low speeds. But he estimated that Starlink's growth rate has slowed since early 2026. He gave no figure.

OTE's answer is 5G FWA, fixed wireless internet over the 5G network. "Essentially, we use Fixed Wireless Access to defend against Starlink's momentum," he said, describing it as "a temporary solution" until fibre arrives. OTE's FWA connections reached 134,355 at the end of September 2026, up from 62,098 a year earlier.

*OTE sees first-hand where it loses and wins customers. How much weight should a direct competitor's estimate carry?*

## 4. Why the two pictures don't clash

One picture is about the size of the base, the other about how fast it grows. A subscriber base can keep rising while its growth rate falls. So both may be right.

One more data point suggests the market in areas without fibre may be maturing overall: OTE's own net FWA additions fell from about 19,000 in Q2 to about 15,500 in Q3. We don't know whether that is down to Starlink, to PPC, or to the fact that those who wanted an alternative have already switched.

*If both FWA and Starlink are growing more slowly, who wins the remaining customers?*

## 5. What will settle it

The next checkpoints are OTE's full-year 2026 results (February 2027), EETT's fixed broadband data, and the ground stations coming online in 2027.

**The picture today:** Starlink has a solid place in the Greek market, with more than 100,000 subscribers by estimates, and its pace, according to OTE, has slowed. The operators' answer runs through 5G and, in the longer term, fibre.
