**INSIGHT · TELECOM & FIBRE** · Series: Fibre in Greece, in three questions (2/3)

# Fibre costs about the same as VDSL. About one in three homes that can get it have it

The fibre networks keep growing and, at several operators, a fibre plan is now priced close to VDSL or below it.
Take-up is rising too, but more slowly than the networks. This piece looks at that gap and at the reasons it might have, without claiming to know which one matters most.

---

## 1. The networks are there

At the end of June 2026 Greece had **1,231,339 active FTTH lines** out of 4,278,534 fixed broadband lines, or **28.78%** (EETT, published 01/09/2026). Eighteen months earlier, at the end of 2024, there were 816,672 (18.68%). At the end of 2025 there were 1,056,049 (24.52%). EETT links crossing 30% to its next regulatory step.

The build-out has gone faster than that. OTE reports **2,221,289 fibre homes passed** in Q2 2026, up from 1,874,548 (OTE results, 29/07/2026). DEI FiberGrid reports 2.3 million homes passed, of which more than 1.5 million are ready for service (Capital, 17/10/2026). Nova's CTO put fibre availability close to 70% (Advanced Telecoms Summit, 29/09/2026, via Euro2day).

*Where you live or work, is fibre already at the door?*

## 2. The price is no longer the barrier it was

On 24-month contracts, checked on 25/10/2026:

| Plan | Vodafone (with TV Start) | Nova | Telekom |
|---|---|---|---|
| VDSL 50 | €26.10 | – | – |
| Fibre 100 | €23.80 | €21 | – |
| Fibre 300 | €27.10 | €23 | €24.90 |
| Fibre 1 Gbps | €30.35 | €31 | €33.90 |

In Vodafone's list, fibre 100 is priced below VDSL 50, and fibre 300 is €1 above it. Vodafone's fibre prices are offer prices for 12 months and can then rise by up to €3. An earlier Telekom price list (February 2026) had VDSL 50 at €22.90 and VDSL 100 at €24.90, the same as Telekom fibre 300 today. That list may have changed since.

So for many households the monthly cost of moving from VDSL to fibre is small, or zero.

*When did you last compare what you pay with what fibre would cost?*

## 3. About one in three

Comparing EETT's active lines at the end of June 2026 with the operators' homes-passed figures, **about one in three homes that can get fibre have taken it** (GR Wire calculation). The operators' own figures point the same way. Nova's CTO gave adoption at **30–32%** against availability close to 70% (29/09/2026). OTE reports **686,831 fibre customers**, up from 470,368 (+46%). It also says **54% of OTE customers who can get fibre have taken it** (29/07/2026). That second figure covers OTE's existing customers only, which is why it is higher.

Buildings tell a similar story. Of 1,439,658 buildings passed, 649,698 (45.1%) have at least one active fibre line. A building covered by more than one network is counted once per network (EETT, end-June 2026).

*Does one in three match what you see in your street or your building?*

## 4. Why? Some possible reasons

There is no single measured answer. These are possibilities, not findings:

- **Inside the building.** Fibre "at the door" is not always fibre in the flat. Wiring through a shared staircase or riser can need agreement among neighbours, a site visit and an appointment.
- **Not knowing the price has moved.** Many people chose their plan when fibre was clearly more expensive. If the comparison is not repeated, the old picture stays.
- **The effort of switching.** A running 24-month contract, a technician visit, a day at home, a new router. Each step is small. Together they are a reason to wait.
- **"What I have works."** At the end of 2025, VDSL was still the largest technology at 46.2% of lines. 46.3% of all lines were already at 100 Mbps or more (EETT broadband report, end-2025 data, posted August 2026). For a household happy with VDSL, the difference may not be obvious.

Probably several of these apply at once, and in different proportions in a city flat, a village house or a small office.

*If you could get fibre and haven't switched, what is the main reason?*

## 5. Next: is the speed actually felt?

nPerf measured an average fixed download speed of **115.89 Mbps** in Q3 2026, against 95.2 Mbps for the whole of 2025. The periods differ, so the comparison is indicative (via InfoCom.gr, 20/10/2026). Part 3 of the series asks whether people feel that in daily use: how much depends on the router, the Wi-Fi in the home and the plan tier, and how much on the line itself.

*After moving to fibre, did you notice the difference, and where?*
